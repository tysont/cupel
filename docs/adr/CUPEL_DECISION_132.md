# CUPEL_DECISION_132: Protect-first targets in `survey`

**Date:** 2026-10-19 · **Status:** accepted · **Phase:** P9.3

## Context

ADR-062 gave `survey` `rankings`: one dimension per per-target metric, each listing its ten highest values, independent of every other dimension. The owner's P9 ruling (follow-up item 3) asks for protect-first ordering: each target paired with its mutation score (and coverage where mutation is absent), and a complex, cloned, or high-CRAP function that no test protects ranked as something to protect (write or strengthen tests) before anything to simplify (refactor), because refactoring unprotected code is unsafe. `survey`'s `next_actions` must follow the same order. Nothing in ADR-062 or ADR-061 answers:

1. Where a target's protection comes from: `tests.mutation` measured only `survivors` per function and `tests.coverage` only `uncovered_lines`, neither a ratio, and nothing measured how many tests run a function.
2. How a dimension where lower is worse (a mutation score) ranks; ADR-062 §4 deferred it to "a direction on `MetricSpec` when it lands".
3. What makes a symbol a target, and when it is to be protected rather than simplified: every function is measured, but "complex" and "unprotected" need thresholds.
4. How targets are joined across dimensions, ordered, and capped, and what the envelope carries.
5. How `next_actions` change for `survey`, and what a suppressed finding does.

## Options

**Protection values: read facts in the CLI, or measurements the checks declare.** `survey` could read `MutationOutcomes` and `CoverageMap` itself, but that duplicates what the checks compute (equivalent survivors, the symbols in scope) and puts check knowledge in the CLI, which ADR-062's consequence ("a check joins `survey` by declaring a dimension and calling `measure`") rules out. Per-symbol measurements the checks already know how to make keep the join generic; a plugin check can declare the same.

**Declaring a protection metric: a direction field, a role field, or both.** A `direction: higher | lower` field on every `MetricSpec` would change every metric of every manifest and every `describe` golden, and still would not say which value is the mutation score. A field naming which protection value a metric is (`mutation-score`, `coverage`, `tests`) says both: a protection metric is lower-is-weaker by definition, so it also fixes the ranking direction. Absent on every other metric (`x-cupel-optional`, as `settings` is), it leaves every existing manifest's JSON unchanged.

**Thresholds: new settings, or the checks' own findings.** A protect threshold (mutation score below 0.8?) and a simplify threshold (cyclomatic above 10?) as `survey` settings would duplicate the limits `static.complexity`, `static.crap`, `static.clones`, `tests.coverage`, and `tests.mutation` already apply and configure in `[checks."<id>"]`. The findings are those thresholds applied: a symbol with a `write_test` or `strengthen_assertion` finding needs protecting; one with `reduce_complexity` or `refactor_extract` needs simplifying. Using the action kind rather than finding kinds keeps it generic: a plugin's finding with those actions joins too, and `crap.high` (whose action is `write_test`) is a protect finding, as a high-CRAP function is by definition under-tested complexity.

**Ordering within protect and simplify: a weighted score, or protection then the metric.** A weighted sum of normalized metrics needs weights nobody can defend. Ordering by protection strength and, at equal strength, by the target's best rank in any other dimension is explainable in one sentence and needs no scale across dimensions (a cyclomatic of 15 and a CRAP of 40 are not comparable values; first place in either is).

**Suppressions: ignore them in rankings, or apply them first.** Rankings are computed in `survey`'s verb, before grading applies suppressions. Ignoring suppression would make an accepted `complexity.high` still recommend simplifying its symbol. Applying the repository's suppressions in the verb before ranking is idempotent (`apply_suppressions` skips findings already suppressed and de-duplicates expiry reports), so grading applying them again changes nothing.

## Decision

1. **`MetricSpec.protection: mutation-score | coverage | tests`**, optional and absent unless set (`x-cupel-optional`; plugins may set it). A protection metric is a per-symbol ranking dimension where lower is weaker. `tests.mutation` declares and measures `mutation_score` (killed / (killed + survived) over the function's mutants, equivalent survivors left out as the check's score leaves them; not measured when none was scored) and `tests` (distinct tests run against the function's mutants, 0 when every mutant was uncovered); `tests.coverage` declares and measures `line_coverage` (covered / instrumented lines). `survivors` and `uncovered_lines` stay as they were.
2. **Every ranking dimension gains `worst: highest | lowest`**: `lowest` for a protection metric, `highest` otherwise; entries are the `top` worst values, ties by display name, file, and line as before. "1 is the highest value" becomes "1 is the worst value".
3. **`rankings` gains `recommended` and `targets`.** A target is a symbol (display name and structural hash) named by an unsuppressed finding whose action is `write_test` or `strengthen_assertion` (protect) or `reduce_complexity` or `refactor_extract` (simplify). Each target is `{rank, recommend: protect | simplify | both, lang, target: {display, symbol, location}, protection: {mutation_score, coverage, tests}, values: [{check, metric, value, rank}], findings: [id]}`: `protection` from its protection measurements (null where none), `values` its value and rank (among every target the dimension measured) in each dimension that measured it, in dimension order, `findings` the protect and simplify findings in envelope order, `location` the measured symbol's span when a dimension measured it, else the first finding's location. Findings without a symbol (a clone cluster names its first member) recommend nothing.
4. **Order, protect-first:** targets to protect (`protect` or `both`) before `simplify`; then the weakest protection, which is the mutation score, else coverage, with an unmeasured protection weakest of all; then the best rank in any dimension that is not a protection metric (no such value last); then display name, file, and line. `recommended` counts every target; `targets` lists the first `top` (10).
5. **`next_actions` with rankings** (only `survey` has them): for each target in order, the most urgent unsuppressed candidate action that protects it (`write_test`, `strengthen_assertion`) and names one of its findings; then, for each target in order, the most urgent that simplifies it (`reduce_complexity`, `refactor_extract`); then every other action ranked as ADR-061 ranks them. De-duplication by kind and location and the cap of ten are unchanged, so a target's actions are its findings' actions and are not repeated below. `check`'s `next_actions` are unchanged.
6. **`survey` applies suppressions before ranking**, so a suppressed finding recommends nothing; grading applies them again with no effect.
7. **Text:** after the dimensions, `targets  N recommended, protect first`, one line per target (rank, recommendation, display name, `mutation · coverage · tests` with `-` for null, language, location, hash), and beneath it its values (`check.metric value (#rank)`) and finding IDs. `describe <check>` text shows `protection <metric>` on a protection metric's row.

## Consequences

- An agent reading `survey` gets one ordered list of what to do, with its evidence; the per-dimension lists stay unchanged apart from `worst`, so nothing a consumer read is removed. The run schema gains `worst`, `recommended`, `targets`, and their types; the plugin schema gains the optional `protection`.
- At `--depth quick` no protection is measured: targets come from the static findings, every protection is null, and protect targets are those `crap.high` and `tests.quality`/`tests.assertion-density` findings name. From `--depth standard` mutation and coverage fill `protection`. `survey` never infers "unprotected" from a missing measurement alone; only a finding says what to do.
- A target's thresholds are its checks' settings, so tightening `[checks."static.complexity"] cyclomatic_max` turns more functions into simplify targets without a `survey` setting.
- Clone clusters name only their first member, so only that member becomes a `refactor_extract` target; the other members appear in the `cluster_size` dimension and in the finding's evidence.
- Runs stored before this change lack `worst`, `recommended`, and `targets`; reading such a stored `survey` run as a baseline fails to deserialize. No release has shipped, so no stored run outside this repository's own `.cupel/` exists; a `#[serde(default)]` on the three fields would read them if that changes.
- The `survey` goldens gain `worst` on every dimension and `recommended`/`targets` in `survey/<lang>.json`; `survey`'s `next_actions` order changes wherever a target has a finding (they are asserted against `cupel_core::next_actions`, not stored).
